# Logo Intake: Review First, Ask Only Gaps

Use this bank as an internal coverage check after reading all accessible materials and relevant conversation context. The questions are not a required questionnaire.

## Answer map

For each ID record the answer or answered portion, supporting filename/page/URL or conversation statement, status, and any remaining gap. Explicit statements are stronger evidence than an inferred visual impression. Do not invent a source for an inference.

Keep current advisor instructions ahead of older collateral. Explicitly stated brand rules can be reused without asking for the same facts again. An observed logo, font, or color does not by itself establish a requirement to retain it. Unresolved business wording or contradictory rules need a targeted question; optional stylistic preferences can be explored as proposals.

## Question bank

1. **Name and wording:** What exact firm name should appear, and is there an approved tagline? Extract exact spelling and capitalization first. An existing tagline is not automatically required for every logo.
2. **Scope and continuity:** Is this a new logo or a refresh, and which elements must stay? The user's request may already settle this. Ask about retention only when undecided and relevant.
3. **Ideal clients:** Who are your ideal clients, and what life stage are they in? Use positioning from the guide or site. Do not request individual client records.
4. **Practice focus:** What services define this practice? Use the actual supplied scope, such as retirement-income planning, investment management, comprehensive planning, or insurance.
5. **Distinction:** Why do those clients choose this practice? Extract concrete differences; do not treat generic claims such as "trusted" as proof of a unique position.
6. **Personality:** Which three qualities should the brand convey? Reuse explicit brand personality. Treat visual impressions as proposed interpretations when no rule exists.
7. **Visual requirements:** Which colors, fonts, or visual elements should remain or be avoided? Read brand specifications first; ask only missing restrictions or redesign choices.
8. **Name story:** Does the name have a meaning that should inform the mark? Use a documented story. Skip this question if it would not materially change the concepts.
9. **References:** Which logos do you like, and what about them appeals to you? Analyze supplied references before asking. Translate preferences into design attributes; avoid copying another firm's mark.
10. **Uses:** Where will the logo appear most often? Reuse stated website, workshop, stationery, packet, pen, signage, or apparel needs. These determine layouts and reproduction checks.
11. **Firm requirements:** Is there firm-required wording, visual guidance, or a review process? Reuse instructions already provided. An absent policy is an open item, not permission to invent a seal or affiliation.

## Conversation rules

- Briefly summarize established answers and ask one to three remaining questions at a time.
- Ask about a conflict with the sources named, rather than repeating the full question.
- If a question has multiple parts, ask only the missing part.
- Accept unavailable materials; do not keep requesting them after the advisor says they do not exist.
- When the advisor is unsure, offer a few relevant choices or a labeled proposal.
- When answers are already sufficient, skip the interview and begin the design brief.
- State a consequential assumption once; retain its status in the brief until resolved.
