# Advisor Branding Rules Output Outline

Use this to plan the material review and organize `Advisor-branding-rules.md` and `Advisor-branding-rules.pdf`. It is not a questionnaire to send before scanning the supplied materials. Keep every section useful to an agent making marketing assets; include enough detail and visual references to reproduce the brand consistently.

Put the practice name, date, version, and draft or reviewed status at the top of both editions. Distinguish advisor-confirmed rules, explicit supplied specifications, observed patterns, and proposals. Maintain one set of rules across both formats.

## Instructions for agents

Explain how to apply the guide to writing, design, and marketing tasks. Follow the advisor's current instructions when they change the guide. Keep unconfirmed proposals separate from established rules and ask about an open item only when it affects the requested asset. The guide does not itself authorize publishing, contacting clients, or approve marketing.

## Practice identity, mission, and values

Record the practice name, advisor's stated role, location or service area, website, and relevant confirmed contact information. Include the mission and values found in the materials or supplied during the gap conversation. Translate values into useful behavior where supported: for example, a value of clarity may mean explaining unfamiliar terms rather than using jargon. Identify newly proposed mission wording or values as proposals, not established company statements.

## Services and ideal client

List the services the practice actually provides and how to describe them. Describe the best-fit client's life circumstances, concerns, goals, decision drivers, and relevant qualifications when known. Distinguish the advisor using the skill from the clients they serve. Include exclusions only when supported. Do not invent services, licenses, credentials, or asset thresholds.

## Positioning, messages, and voice

Give a concise positioning statement and a few supported key messages or differentiators. Describe the tone, practical writing rules, preferred phrases, and language to avoid. Include brief voice examples if useful; label newly written examples as proposals. Keep messaging tied to the recorded services and client concerns without promising results.

## Color palette

Show actual swatches in the PDF. In both formats, record each color's name, HEX code when known, role, source, and status. Include primary, secondary, accent, background, and text colors only when established or clearly proposed. Add usage guidance, such as which color is used for headings or calls to action, and which combinations appear in approved materials. Mark sampled image colors as approximate observations; do not promote them to official specifications without confirmation. Do not invent print or color-space values absent from the sources.

## Typography and layout

Record verified typefaces, weights, and hierarchy when known, and observed styling when exact fonts are unavailable. Cover heading/body treatment, spacing, visual density, graphic motifs, and useful layout rules supported by the materials. Clearly label fallback font or layout suggestions as proposals.

## Logos

Embed previews of selected logo variants in the PDF. Include relative image links or previews in the Markdown. For each variant, record a stable asset ID, filename, purpose, source, approved backgrounds, and supplied usage restrictions. Explain which variant to use for common applications when that is known. Preserve proportions, original files, and any known clear-space or minimum-size requirements. Do not manufacture exact logo specifications or redesign a supplied mark.

## Photos and imagery

Embed available selected photos in the PDF with clear captions. Include relative image links in the Markdown and references to original assets. Record stable asset IDs, filenames, known subjects, purpose, source, and any supplied usage or cropping limitations. Cover headshots, team, office, and other approved brand imagery when available. Distinguish production-ready originals from screenshots or low-resolution references. Treat unknown rights, subjects, or approvals as unresolved; do not fabricate photos or assume a brand-new visual direction.

## Calls to action and required wording

Record the preferred next step and actual supplied links or contact routes. Include any supplied approved wording or disclosures with their source. Mark missing contact details when relevant rather than inventing them. Keep relevant restrictions on claims and promises concise; do not create a generic regulatory checklist or label the guide compliance-approved.

## Asset inventory, sources, and open items

List the reviewed materials, website URLs and access dates, selected assets, and any unavailable sources. Use portable filenames or asset IDs, not private absolute paths. Link original assets relatively from `brand-assets/`. Summarize meaningful conflicts and the advisor's resolutions, along with the remaining decisions or missing materials. Missing visual categories should say "not supplied" or "not established" rather than disappearing or being filled with placeholders presented as assets.

## Reuse prompt

> Use the attached Advisor-branding-rules when creating marketing for my practice. Follow the mission, audience, voice, colors, logo, and photo rules. For this task, help me [describe the marketing asset].

The PDF should combine the written rules with labeled visual reference pages. It is not an image-only mood board or a text-only list of asset filenames. Keep original assets alongside both files so a future agent can create assets using the actual files.
